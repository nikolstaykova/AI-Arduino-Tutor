Privacy policy

Last updated {{UPDATED}}

CircuitQuest is a free, hobby learning app that teaches electronics by building Arduino circuits. This page explains exactly what it stores and why.

What we collect

  • Your Google account basics — when you sign in with Google we receive your name, email address and profile picture. We use them to show who you are, to keep your progress in your account and to label lessons you create. We never see your Google password.
  • Your progress — levels finished, stars, XP, streaks and your settings (like beginner/advanced).
  • Lessons you create — the circuit, code and steps, your request or the tutorial link you used, and your name as the maker. Created lessons are shared with everyone who uses the app, with your name shown on them.
  • Your Anthropic API key, if you connect one — it is checked with Anthropic, then stored encrypted; only its last 4 characters are ever shown. It is used only to create lessons you ask for. You can remove it at any time (Create → Disconnect).

We don't use advertising or tracking cookies, and we don't sell or rent your information. The app stores a sign-in session and a few preferences (like the parts you picked) in your browser.

Who processes it

  • Supabase — sign-in and the database that holds accounts, progress and lessons.
  • Render — hosts the app.
  • Anthropic — when you create a lesson, your request (and the tutorial page's text, if you give a link) is sent to Anthropic's Claude to write it, on your own API key.
  • Google — sign-in only.

Google user data

CircuitQuest only requests your basic Google profile (name, email, picture). Its use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. It is not used for ads, not sold, and not shared except with the services listed above to run the app.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data is kept while your account exists. To delete your account, progress and saved key — or to have your name removed from lessons you created — {{CONTACT}}. We'll do it and confirm.

Children

CircuitQuest is meant for learners of all ages, but signing in requires a Google account, so younger learners should use it with a parent's or teacher's account and supervision.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too.
